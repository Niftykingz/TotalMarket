Crude oil: Brent tops $110 for a moment, settles at $108.79 as Hormuz risk lingers

Oil hit a five-month high on tanker attacks before easing, and it was rising again in early Asian trade.

21 Oct 2026, 8:48 am IST2 min readHDFC Sky: US market close
By Claude (AI) for TotalMarket · automated update

Oil prices swung sharply. Brent crude, the global benchmark, briefly climbed above $110 a barrel on Tuesday, its highest in five months, then settled 0.69% lower at $108.79. US crude (WTI, December contract) ended at $98.28. Early on Wednesday Brent was up about 1.1% at $110.03, according to Reuters.

The reason is the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow sea route between Iran and Oman that carries a large share of the world's oil. Reports of tanker attacks there keep a 'risk premium' in prices: buyers pay extra today in case supply gets disrupted tomorrow. Prices eased during the US session as traders focused on strong company earnings and lower bond yields.

India imports more than 85% of the crude it uses, so every $10 rise in oil costs the country billions of dollars a year and pushes up the import bill. Worked example: if Brent stays near $110 instead of $100, oil companies' costs per barrel rise about 10%, and that pressure eventually shows up in fuel, transport and plastic prices if it lasts.

Key takeaway: oil near $110 is the biggest outside risk for Indian inflation and the rupee this week.

◍Global impact

High oil raises inflation worldwide and makes it harder for central banks like the Fed to cut rates; energy stocks were the weakest US sector on Tuesday as prices eased.

▲Impact on India

Costlier oil widens India's trade deficit, weakens the rupee and hurts oil-using sectors like paints, airlines and tyres, while helping upstream producers such as ONGC and Oil India.
