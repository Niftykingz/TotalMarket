Viral market posts fact-checked: WhatsApp tips, ‘156-year’ crash call, Bitcoin cycle

The biggest money claims racing across X and WhatsApp this month, and what the data actually says about each one.

20 Oct 2026, 9:10 pm IST3 min readBusiness Today: Nithin Kamath on WhatsApp stock manipulation
By Boss Piggy AI · TotalMarket AI market desk

Big claims travel fastest on social media, and markets are no exception. This month's most-shared money posts range from a genuine warning by a broker's founder to a misleading graphic about an Indian 'collapse' and crash predictions built on valuation records and Bitcoin cycles. We checked the most talked-about ones against regulators, data and reporting.

The post with the most real-world weight came from Zerodha co-founder Nithin Kamath on 20 October, who wrote that it was 'crazy' people still fall for stock tips on WhatsApp, Telegram and SMS. It landed days after the Enforcement Directorate searched 29 premises in six cities, including Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Surat and Bhavnagar, over alleged manipulation of RM Drip and Sprinklers Systems and R&B Denims. Investigators say WhatsApp groups such as 'Multibagger Stocks' pushed daily buy calls from December 2025 to February 2026, growing from about 3,400 to over one lakh members. When the groups were deleted, both stocks hit lower circuits and reportedly lost about 70% in days. Trading accounts worth about ₹200 crore have been frozen, according to Business Today.

Other viral claims hold up less well. A graphic shared by a US commentator and amplified by a US Labor Department official on about 9 October said Indian stocks 'collapsed' over new US green-card curbs on IT firms, but users pointed out the Indian market had closed hours before the announcement and IT shares rebounded the next session. A widely shared warning that the US market is about to do something 'for the first time in 156 years' refers to the S&P 500 Shiller P/E, which stood at 41.67 on 5 October, close to but still below its December 1999 record of 44.19. A viral Bitcoin theory of 1,064-day rallies followed by 364-day falls pointed to early October 2026 as a turning point; Bitcoin was about $82,440 on 19 October, roughly 35% below its October 2025 peak of $126,198, but past patterns do not guarantee future moves. Older favourites keep resurfacing too: a post claiming ₹1 lakh turned into ₹44 lakh in five minutes on Sensex options during the 27 August closing auction was described by experts as hypothetical, and PIB Fact Check keeps flagging AI deepfake videos of the Prime Minister and Finance Minister 'endorsing' investment platforms that promise lakhs from a ₹21,000–22,000 deposit.

◍Global impact

Valuation and crypto-cycle posts shape sentiment worldwide; a US market near record valuations and Bitcoin well off its peak mean viral crash calls get attention quickly, whether or not they come true.

▲Impact on India

Pump-and-dump groups have already left over one lakh Indian retail investors holding stocks that crashed about 70%. Misleading 'collapse' graphics and deepfake schemes mainly hurt small savers, which is why SEBI and PIB keep issuing warnings.

Why it mattersViral posts can move small stocks and pull savers into schemes before facts catch up. SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey has warned investors against anonymous tips, finfluencers and 'guaranteed' returns.
