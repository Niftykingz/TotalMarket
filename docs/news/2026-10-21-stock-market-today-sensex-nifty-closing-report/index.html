Stock market today: Nifty ends at 22,811 after 450-point swing; smallcaps sink 2.37%, IT jumps 2.9%

An 800-point Sensex rally faded into a second straight loss as oil near $110 and broad selling hit mid and small caps; IT was the only sector in the green.

21 Oct 2026, 8:20 pm IST4 min readBusiness Standard (live blog, close)

It was a day of two halves. The Sensex jumped as much as 829 points in the morning, to 74,682.19, and the Nifty 50 touched 23,120.90, as Asian markets bounced and money poured into the Jio IPO. By 3 pm both had fallen more than 1.8% from their highs, before the closing auction trimmed the loss. The Sensex ended 143.21 points (0.19%) lower at 73,709.69 and the Nifty 50 fell 77.60 points (0.34%) to 22,811.35, its second straight fall.

Why the turn? Brent crude stayed near $110 a barrel, keeping inflation worries alive, and US bond yields remain above 5%. Heavyweights HDFC Bank, Bajaj Finance and Eternal together dragged the Sensex down by more than 300 points, Business Standard reported. Selling was far wider than the headline numbers suggest: on NSE, 2,988 shares fell and only 608 rose. The Nifty Midcap 100 lost 1.58% to a six-month low and the Nifty Smallcap 100 fell 2.37% to a four-month low. India VIX, the market's fear gauge, rose about 6% to 15.7.

IT was the one bright spot. The Nifty IT index rose 2.88% to an eight-month high, with every stock in it ending higher; Infosys (+3.3%), HCL Technologies (+3.2%) and Tech Mahindra (+3.1%) led the Nifty. The rupee also strengthened 48 paise to a six-week high of 94.43 per dollar on Jio IPO inflows and RBI support. After market hours, Eternal reported second-quarter profit of ₹125 crore, up 92%, on revenue up 78% to ₹24,138 crore.

Key takeaway: when the Nifty falls only 0.34% but five shares fall for every one that rises, most investors' portfolios had a worse day than the index shows.

What happened

The Nifty 50 closed 0.34% lower at 22,811.35 after swinging about 450 points between its high and low. Mid and small caps fell 1.6–2.4%, while IT stocks rose 2.9%.

Why it matters

Five shares fell for every one that rose on NSE, so most investors lost money even though the Nifty barely moved. Oil near $110 and US yields above 5% are the main pressures.
