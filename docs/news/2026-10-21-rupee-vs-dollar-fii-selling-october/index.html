Rupee at 94.92 as FIIs pull ₹49,900 crore from Indian shares in October

A softer dollar overnight helps, but steady foreign selling and costly oil keep the rupee near 95.

21 Oct 2026, 8:47 am IST2 min read5paisa: FII DII data
By Boss Piggy AI · TotalMarket AI market desk

The rupee last closed at 94.92 per US dollar on Monday, 3 paise weaker, before Tuesday's Dussehra holiday. It reopens today with mixed signals: the dollar index, which measures the dollar against six major currencies, fell 0.26% to 101.71 overnight, which usually helps the rupee, but Brent oil is back near $110.

The bigger pressure is money leaving India. FIIs, foreign institutional investors such as big overseas funds, sold a net ₹1,148.6 crore of Indian shares on Monday and about ₹49,918 crore so far in October, according to provisional data compiled by 5paisa. DIIs, domestic institutional investors such as Indian mutual funds and insurers, bought ₹3,201 crore on Monday and about ₹51,369 crore this month. When foreign funds sell, they convert rupees into dollars, which pushes the rupee down.

Worked example: a student paying $20,000 in US college fees needs about ₹18.98 lakh at 94.92. Every 1-rupee fall in the rupee adds ₹20,000 to that bill, while an exporter earning $20,000 gets ₹20,000 more.

Key takeaway: watch FII flows and oil; together they decide most of the rupee's daily move.

◍Global impact

A slightly weaker dollar after softer US yields gives emerging-market currencies some relief, but oil-importing countries like India remain under pressure.

▲Impact on India

A weak rupee makes imports such as oil, electronics and gold costlier and raises the cost of foreign travel and education, while it helps exporters like IT and pharma companies.
