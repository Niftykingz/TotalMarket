IPO school: learn the basics

What is an IPO?

An initial public offering is the first time a company sells its shares to the public. After the IPO, the shares trade on NSE and BSE like any other stock.

Price band and cut-off price

The company sets a range, say ₹660–693. Investors bid within it; the final price is usually the top of the band. Retail investors can tick 'cut-off' to accept whatever final price is set.

Lot size and minimum investment

You apply in fixed bundles called lots. If the lot is 21 shares and the top price is ₹693, the minimum application is 21 × ₹693 = ₹14,553.

Fresh issue vs offer for sale (OFS)

In a fresh issue the company creates new shares and keeps the money. In an OFS, existing owners sell their shares and the money goes to them, not the company.

Anchor investors

Big institutions that buy a part of the issue one working day before it opens. Half their shares are locked for 30 days and the rest for 90 days, so their lock-in end dates can bring selling.

QIB, NII and retail quotas

The issue is split by investor type: QIBs (mutual funds, insurers, foreign funds), NIIs or HNIs (individuals applying above ₹2 lakh) and retail (up to ₹2 lakh). Each quota has its own subscription number.

What does '10x subscribed' mean?

Investors applied for 10 shares for every share on offer. When retail is oversubscribed, lots are allotted by a computerised lottery, so many applicants get nothing.

Grey market premium (GMP)

An unofficial price quoted by dealers before listing. It is unregulated, not recognised by SEBI or the exchanges, and often wrong. Treat it as gossip, not data.

ASBA and UPI mandate

When you apply, the money is only blocked in your bank account, not taken. If you get no shares, the block is released; if you do, only the allotted amount is debited.

Red flags to check in the RHP

The Red Herring Prospectus is the official offer document. Look at the use of proceeds, how much is OFS, debt levels, profit history, pending legal cases and how the price compares with listed peers.

SME vs mainboard IPOs

SME IPOs are small companies on the NSE Emerge or BSE SME platforms, with bigger minimum lots and thinner trading. They carry higher risk than mainboard IPOs.
