Lesson: why time matters more than amount

  1. Start with an example. Say you invest ₹5,000 every month for 10 years and earn 12% a year. You put in ₹6.00 lakh and it grows to about ₹11.62 lakh.
  2. Now only change the time. Keep ₹5,000 a month but continue for 20 years. You put in ₹12.00 lakh, double the money, but it grows to about ₹49.96 lakh, roughly 4.3 times as much.
  3. Why does that happen? This is compounding: the returns you earn start earning returns of their own. In the later years most of the growth comes from past gains, not from your new instalments.
  4. What to take away. Starting early, and staying invested through ups and downs, does more of the work than a bigger monthly amount started late.

Remember: 12% is an assumption, not a promise. Equity funds can lose money in some years. Check a fund's past record and costs, and speak to a SEBI-registered adviser if you are unsure.

Questions people ask

What is a SIP?

A systematic investment plan (SIP) means investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month instead of a lump sum. Each instalment buys units at that day's price.

How is SIP return calculated?

This calculator uses the standard future-value formula with monthly compounding: FV = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where P is the monthly amount, i is the yearly return ÷ 12 and n is the number of months.

Is the SIP return guaranteed?

No. Equity mutual fund returns go up and down every year. The calculator assumes a steady return only to show how compounding works; real results will differ.

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up SIP raises the monthly amount every year, for example by 10% when your salary grows. Try the step-up box in the calculator to see the difference.
